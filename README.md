CS 255: System Analysis and Design - Reflection
For the DriverPass project, I designed a cloud-hosted scheduling and management platform for business owner Liam and IT officer Ian. They needed a system capable of handling online student registration, two-hour driving lesson scheduling matched to specific cars, and DMV-synced practice tests. I did particularly well at translating their strict scheduling constraints into clear UML Activity and Sequence diagrams. If I could revise one section of my system design document, I would expand the UML Class Diagram to include granular data attributes for the external payment gateway, which would better illustrate that API integration. I interpreted the users' needs by analyzing the interview transcript and converting their core pain points into precise functional requirements. Considering these needs is essential because a technically sound system is useless if it fails to solve the client's actual business problems. Moving forward, my approach to software design will remain focused on iterative modeling. I plan to consistently utilize UML diagrams and Agile requirements gathering to ensure the underlying technical architecture aligns perfectly with the client's operational goals before development begins.
